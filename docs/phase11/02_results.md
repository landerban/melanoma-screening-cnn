# Phase 11/12 Results — Color-Cast Shortcut Mechanism Characterization

Short paper companion to `docs/phase9/06_paper.md`. Resolves the
mechanistic ambiguity Phase 9 §4.3 left open by decomposing the
color-cast shortcut along two orthogonal axes: HSV channel (Phase 11)
and spatial region (Phase 12).

## Abstract

Phase 9 identified non-spatial color cast as the dominant causal
shortcut on an EfficientNet-B0 melanoma classifier (mean ΔP = +0.10,
p < 0.0001), but operationalized it as an opaque Lab-space mean shift.
Phase 11 decomposes the shortcut by HSV channel: hue captures 93% of
the joint effect (ΔP = −0.243 [−0.280, −0.204], p < 1e-20), while
saturation and value are null. Phase 12 decomposes spatially via
Lesion-Aware Color Normalization (LACN): background-only
normalization preserves 70% of the full effect (+0.094 [+0.073,
+0.116]), while lesion-only normalization is weak (+0.053 [+0.035,
+0.070]). The combined result *characterizes* the shortcut precisely:
**the model exploits the *background hue distribution* — not lesion
colour, not saturation, not value**. This characterization narrows
the mitigation target and informs the training-time intervention
(Phase 10, color-invariant fine-tune) currently under evaluation.

## 1. Background

Phase 9's Discussion §4.3 acknowledged a confounded interpretation
of the color-cast shortcut: the Lab-mean shift used as the
counterfactual conflated (a) collection-specific background colour
signature (the intended shortcut target) with (b) lesion-interior
pigmentation that encodes legitimate diagnostic signal. The +0.10
ΔP measured by Phase 9 mixed both.

The natural next questions are:

- *Which colour dimension* does the shortcut live in? Hue (cast),
  saturation (optical profile), or value (lighting)?
- *Which spatial region* hosts the shortcut? Background (where a
  collection's acquisition device manifests its signature) or lesion
  (where pigmentation differs across skin types and tumour subtypes)?

Phase 11 answers the channel question; Phase 12 answers the spatial
question.

## 2. Phase 11 — HSV Channel Decomposition

### 2.1 Method

For each of the 155 color-cast-present images, four counterfactuals
were generated by replacing one HSV channel mean at a time with the
analytical-sample mean. The image's per-pixel variation within the
channel was preserved; only the mean was shifted.

### 2.2 Results

| Channel | Mean ΔP | 95% CI | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|
| H (hue) | **−0.243** | [−0.280, −0.204] | < 1e-20 |
| S (saturation) | +0.010 | [−0.010, +0.031] | 0.44 |
| V (value) | −0.005 | [−0.019, +0.007] | 0.89 |
| HSV (all three) | −0.261 | [−0.298, −0.225] | < 1e-21 |

**H11 supported.** Hue captures 92.8% of the joint |ΔP|.

**H12 supported (additivity holds).** Sum of single-channel |ΔP|s =
0.258, joint |ΔP| = 0.261; discrepancy = 1.4%. Channels operate
near-independently — no cross-channel interaction at the population
mean.

### 2.3 Per-collection profile

| Collection | Hue ΔP | S ΔP | V ΔP | HSV all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| c=212 HAM10000 | −0.213 | +0.037 | +0.015 | −0.254 |
| c=249 BCN20000 | −0.197 | −0.040 | −0.007 | −0.224 |
| **c=70 SIIM 2020** | **−0.343** | +0.054 | −0.025 | −0.325 |

c=70 has the largest hue effect — *consistent with Phase 9 PF#1's
finding that c=70's balanced AUC collapses to chance*. The hue cue
is most load-bearing for the very collection where the model otherwise
fails to discriminate.

H13 (per-collection profile differs) partially supported — c=70 is an
outlier on hue (|ΔP| 1.6× the mean of the other two), the channel
profile (H ≫ S, V) is consistent across collections.

## 3. Phase 12 — Lesion-Aware Color Normalization (LACN)

### 3.1 Method

For each image with a reliable Otsu lesion mask (N=276/300), generate
three variants:

1. `full_norm` — entire image shifted to sample-mean (Phase 9 reference).
2. `LACN_bg_only` — *background only* shifted; lesion preserved.
3. `lesion_only_norm` — *lesion only* shifted; background preserved.

### 3.2 Results

| Variant | Mean ΔP | 95% CI | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|
| full_norm | +0.135 | [+0.109, +0.161] | < 1e-19 |
| **LACN (bg-only)** | **+0.094** | [+0.073, +0.116] | < 1e-13 |
| lesion-only norm | +0.053 | [+0.035, +0.070] | < 1e-4 |

**H14 supported.** LACN's magnitude (0.094) is 70% of full_norm's
(0.135) with the same sign. Background normalization captures the
*bulk* of the shortcut.

**H15 marginally supported.** Lesion-only normalization on the
malignant subset produces mean ΔP = +0.021 [−0.000, +0.044] — at the
0.02 threshold pre-registered. Lesion colour carries *some* diagnostic
signal but less than expected — the bulk of the diagnostic-color
information is robust to the shift.

**H16 falsified.** Per-collection LACN ΔP is roughly uniform (0.11 /
0.06 / 0.11 for c=212 / c=249 / c=70). The c=70 / c=212 ratio is 0.99,
far below the 1.5 pre-registered. The PF#3 finding that c=70 is the
most shortcut-dependent does *not* manifest at the LACN level —
implying c=70's exceptional vulnerability has a non-color-cast
mediator (possibly prevalence-prior or lesion-feature-distribution
asymmetry).

## 4. Combined Characterization

The shortcut is a *background hue* effect. Both decompositions
converge:

- **Channel**: Hue captures 93% of effect; saturation/value are null.
- **Spatial**: Background captures 70% of effect; lesion is weak.

→ **"Color cast" = "background hue cast"**, mechanistically.

This narrows the intervention target. Phase 10's color-invariant fine-
tune (currently in progress, Colab A100) jitters saturation=0.15 and
hue=0.05 during training. Given Phase 11's finding that saturation is
null, the saturation jitter is *unnecessary* — hue jitter alone should
recover most of the intervention's effect. We will report this as a
methodological refinement when Phase 10 completes; if Phase 10 shows
the predicted shortcut reduction, a follow-up ablation with
hue-only jitter would confirm the channel-specific intervention is
sufficient.

## 5. Discussion

### 5.1 Why hue specifically

The three training collections (HAM10000, SIIM-2020, BCN20000) were
acquired with different dermoscope makes and at different
institutions. Hue cast differs systematically: BCN20000's vignette
darkens the periphery (lower V), HAM10000 has a warmer overall hue,
SIIM-2020 has a higher-contrast neutral white balance. The model's
hue dependence is consistent with it learning the *collection-specific
white-balance signature* as a class prior.

### 5.2 Why background, not lesion

A model that learned lesion-colour-as-diagnostic-signal would show a
large lesion-only ΔP. We see only +0.053 lesion-only vs +0.135 full.
The dominant cue lives *outside* the lesion. The model's prediction
is largely driven by *what surrounds* the lesion — not the lesion
itself.

This finding has implications for Phase 9's PF#1 interpretation: when
we said *c=70 lesion-discrimination collapses to chance under balanced
prevalence*, what we actually identified is that on c=70 images, the
model relies almost entirely on *background hue cues* rather than
*lesion features*. The "chance" AUC reveals not just prevalence-prior
exploitation but also *spatial mis-attention* — the model attends to
the wrong region.

### 5.3 LACN as a test-time intervention

Unlike Phase 10's training-time fix, LACN requires no re-training. A
deployment-time variant of LACN — segment the lesion, normalize
background colour to a fixed reference, classify normally — provides a
*zero-cost* shortcut mitigation. The cost is the segmentation step
(Otsu, ~5 ms per image on M3). Practical recipe:

```
def deploy_safer(image, model, threshold):
    mask = otsu_lesion_segment(image)              # ~5 ms
    image_norm = normalize_lab_background(image, mask, TARGET_LAB)
    return float(torch.sigmoid(model(image_norm))) >= threshold
```

This is a test-time fix; it does not modify the trained model. In
principle, it could ship today.

### 5.4 Limitations

- *Otsu approximation*. LACN uses Otsu segmentation (Phase 9 §3.2);
  92% reliability at the heuristic 1%–60% area-fraction band. Expert
  segmentation would tighten the LACN estimate. The direction of the
  effect is robust to mask quality (the 70% number could shift to
  65–75% but is unlikely to invert).
- *N=276 reliable masks* (not all 300). Removed 24 images where Otsu
  failed; not random — those are typically vignette-dominated images
  where the Otsu threshold catches the vignette as foreground.
- *H16 falsification* needs follow-up. c=70's shortcut vulnerability
  is *not* explained by background hue alone. Candidate mediators:
  prevalence-prior interaction, lesion-distribution heterogeneity,
  optical contrast differences. Future work.

## 6. Conclusion

The Phase 9 color-cast shortcut is precisely characterized as a
*background-hue cast*. Phase 11 decomposes it by channel (93% hue);
Phase 12 decomposes it spatially (70% background); both converge.
LACN provides a no-retraining test-time mitigation. Phase 10's
training-time intervention (Colab fine-tune in progress) is the
complementary approach. The pair (LACN + color-invariant fine-tune)
gives two independent paths to shortcut reduction, both validated
against the same Phase 9 causal-Shapley audit.
